**Settings → Automations** (the **Automations** card in Settings) lets you build rules in plain words: *when something happens to a
shipment, do something*. An automation can tag a shipment, assign it, change its status, send a
template, notify the team or send it to a webhook.

The page has two tabs: **Automations** (your rules) and **Runs** (what they did).

<Note>
  Automations are turned on company by company. If you do not see **Automations** in Settings,
  contact BeRocker support to have it turned on. Your role also needs permission to view the page.
  Only roles that can manage automations can create, edit, turn on or off, duplicate or delete
  them; everyone else sees the rules read-only.
</Note>

---

## The automations list

The **Automations** card shows how many rules are turned on out of the most your company may have
on at once (*… of … turned on*). Each row shows:

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Automation** | The name (click it to open the rule) and the description. |
| **When** | What starts it. |
| **If** | Its conditions, in short. |
| **Do** | How many actions it runs. |
| **On** | A switch to turn the rule on or off. |
| **Runs** | How many times it ran and when it last ran. |

On each row, **Edit**, **Duplicate** and **Delete** manage the rule.

## Create an automation: When, If and Do

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click New automation">
    On **Settings → Automations**, click **New automation**. The editor opens on the right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    **Name** is required (for example *Tag hot leads and tell Sam*). **Description** is optional.
  </Step>
  <Step title="When: pick what starts it">
    Choose the event under **When**, such as a new shipment, a status change, a customer message, a
    payment or an accepted carrier offer. A line under the list explains the event you picked.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If: add conditions (optional)">
    Under **If**, click **Add condition** and choose a **Field**, an **Operator** and a **Value**.
    With more than one condition, choose **All must match** or **Any can match**. With no conditions
    the rule runs every time (*No conditions: runs every time.*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do: add the actions">
    Under **Do**, use **+ Add an action** and pick what to do. Each choice shows a short description.
    Actions include sending one of your message templates, assigning to a person, changing the
    status, adding or removing a tag, scheduling a call, adding a note, alerting people on the team,
    sending the shipment to a webhook, or waiting before the next step. Fill in each action's
    settings, and use the arrows to change their order. There is a limit on actions per automation,
    shown when you reach it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn it on and save">
    **Turned on** decides whether it runs. Off keeps the automation but never runs it. Click
    **Create** (or **Save** when editing).
  </Step>
</Steps>

If something is missing, the editor lists it under **Fix these before saving** (for example
*Name: give the automation a name.*, *When: pick a trigger.* or *Do: add at least one action.*).

Messages sent by an automation follow the same rules as everything else: never to a customer who
opted out or is blocked, held until their quiet hours end, and never the same template twice in a
day.

### Canvas view

Where your company has it, the editor has a **Form** / **Canvas** switch. **Canvas** draws the same
rule as connected boxes. Click a box to edit that part, drag boxes to arrange them (the layout is
saved with the automation), and use **Auto layout** to tidy them. The form stays the default.

## Test with a shipment before you turn it on

Open a saved automation and scroll to **Test with a shipment**. It answers: *Would this automation
run for a shipment as it is right now? Nothing is sent or changed.*

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter a shipment">
    Type the **Shipment id** and click **Check**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the result">
    You see *It would run for shipment #…* or *It would not run for shipment #…*, the reasons, and,
    when it matches, **It would:** followed by each step it would take.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The check uses the saved version of the automation. If you changed something, save first to test
your changes.

## Turn an automation on or off, duplicate or delete it

- **Turn on or off:** use the switch in the **On** column, or **Turned on** in the editor.
- **Duplicate:** makes a copy of the rule. The copy is turned off until you are ready.
- **Delete:** asks first. The automation stops at once, its run history is deleted with it, and a
  run that is waiting will not finish. This cannot be undone.

## Automation runs: what each rule did and why

**Settings → Automations → Runs** lists every time an event reached an automation, matched or not,
with what it did and why. Runs are kept for a limited number of days, shown on the tab.

Filter by **Any automation**, **Any status** and **Matched or not** (**Matched** or
**Did not match**), and click **Refresh** to reload.

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **When** | How long ago (hover for the exact time). |
| **Automation** | Which rule. |
| **Shipment** | The shipment number and customer. |
| **Event** | What started it. |
| **Status** | **Did not match**, **Running**, **Waiting**, **Done**, **Partly done** or **Failed**. |
| **Matched** | **Yes** or **No**. |
| **Took** | How long it ran. |

Click a run to open its detail: the status, event, customer and time, when a waiting run
**Resumes**, when it **Finished**, the **Conditions** it checked, and the **Steps** with the outcome
of each one.

Every run is also written on the shipment, with the reason it ran or did not.
